# Method

MecCog Step 1 submission.

- **Team:** Groovy
- **Hypothesis:** H2, impaired ARF6-related receptor recycling. "In some types of non-Alzheimer's, non-aged human brain cells, for example astrocytes, APOE4 causes impaired ARF6-related recycling of receptors between the cell surface and the endosome, compared with APOE3."
- **Submission date:** 2026-11-19

---

## 1. Who did the work

A person and an agent (`both`). One human team leader set the task, the scope and the rules, and reviewed the output. One orchestrating agent did the searching, ranking, full-text retrieval and review, and assembled the sheet. It handed per-paper extraction to eight sub-agents running the same model. Each sub-agent got one or two papers and the same written brief (`prompts/EXTRACTION_BRIEF.md`). The orchestrator then checked every sub-agent's rows (section 5) and corrected them before assembly. The human's checks are listed in section 5.

## 2. Model and version

- Orchestrator: `claude-opus-5-5` (Anthropic), run in Claude Code (desktop app).
- Extraction sub-agents (8): `claude-opus-5-5`, run in the same Claude Code session.
- Gap-targeted search rounds 3 to 7, the referee pass and the extraction and review of the round 8 and round 9 papers, and the round 9 screen: `claude-opus-5-5`, in a second Claude Code session started by the orchestrator. It worked from the same written rules and state files, and the orchestrator re-checked its results (row checks, validator, counts) before they were accepted. Row changes from the referee pass were applied only after the team leader approved them.
- Round 10 (search, extraction and review of McColl 2003 and 37777962): the orchestrator.
- Exploratory kinetic model (section 6): `claude-opus-5-5`, in a side Claude Code session; it reads figures already in the evidence set and adds no rows.

## 5. What a human checked

**Automated and agent checks, on 100% of rows:**

- **DOI/PMID:** every DOI matches the DOI PubMed lists for its PMID. A deliberately wrong pair was caught as a negative control.
- **Quote:** every quote is a verbatim substring of the retrieved full text (`scripts/check_rows.py`, with a self-test that catches 6/6 seeded defects).
- **Statistic fields:** shape and provenance pairing is valid.
- **Estimates:** `~` appears only with `estimated_from_figure`, and no P value is estimated.
- **Quote position:** the cited figure is mentioned within 600 characters of the quote (`scripts/quote_near_fig.py`, with a positive and a negative control). Every shortlisted row was read by the orchestrator. In the final run, 17 of 151 checked rows were shortlisted, none of them McColl 2003 (none from the four papers added in the second round, 2 from the paper added in round 3, 1 from round 8, 7 from round 9), and all are legend or Results sentences sitting under the right figure; the round 9 rows quote legends that label the panel by letter only ("C:", "(D)"). The round 8 row is a Cataldo 2000 Results result reported as "data not shown", so it has no figure to sit near. In an earlier run, 1 quote was replaced.
- **Official validator** (`validate_submission.py` from the challenge page): no errors in the Submission sheet.

**Orchestrator review of every sub-agent row:**

- **P values:** exact values printed on a figure panel were recoded to `printed_in_text`.
- **Wrong comparison:** where significance brackets compared ApoE4 with a no-ApoE control rather than with ApoE3, the row now says so. A P value attached to a contrast the row does not describe was set to N/A.
- **Off-topic rows:** three were removed.
- **Figures:** four Zhao 2017 figures were read in the browser to replace codes the sub-agent assigned because it had not seen the images. ## 6. Anything else

- **Hypothesis boundary.**
  - "Receptor recycling" was read as: the surface level, endosomal retention or recycling rate of a cell-surface receptor or transporter (ABCA1, LRP1, ApoER2, NMDA/AMPA receptors, insulin receptor, transferrin receptor).
  - Ligand-uptake and endosome-marker readouts (including early-endosome number, size and volume) are included as class 3 surrogates.
  - **Directness class.** Following the challenge's directness guide, B in H2 is the recycling of receptors between the cell surface and the endosome. Class 1 is kept for the 7 rows that measure that movement itself: biotin recycling and retention time courses, the fraction of receptor held inside the cell, internalisation after insulin, a transferrin pulse-chase, and one abstract that reports recycling inhibited. Rows that measure only surface level are class 3 (B' in the guide), because synthesis or breakdown could also change surface level. This includes the Reelin assays (20547867, 30375977, 34617884), which trigger internalisation and then read surface/total once: the authors describe them as recycling assays, but a single surface level cannot separate slower recycling from faster internalisation. 30 rows moved from class 1 to class 3 on 2026-11-21; no row shows that the recycling is ARF6-related.
  - Fewer early endosomes with APOE4 is not coded as against: H2 does not require early endosomes to swell, and fewer endosomes fits the reduced transferrin uptake seen in the same isogenic cells (42239208). This matters for de Leeuw 2025, where Rab5 endosomes are fewer in young APOE4 male mice (hippocampus, entorhinal cortex) but more in old APOE4 female mice (cortex, hippocampus); none of its 6 rows is coded as against.
  - Endosomal pH (NHE6) is included only as the proposed cause of the recycling block.
  - Only Rawat 2019 links ARF6 itself to APOE4. Its ARF6 evidence is a change in total ARF6 protein and an ARF6-knockdown efflux readout; no ARF6-dependent recycling assay was found anywhere.
- **Evidence gaps.** Five targeted search rounds found nothing new for the three weakest links of the hypothesis: rounds 3 to 7 (section 3). Round 10 did not close any of them. Rounds 6 (public datasets) and 7 (human tissue) are summarised under gap 2. These look like gaps in the published literature, not gaps in the search, and they limit how far the submission can support H2:
  1. **ARF6.** Rawat 2019 is the only paper that links ARF6 to APOE4: more ARF6 protein in APOE4 astrocytes, and an ARF6 knockdown that raises ABCA1 efflux. No paper measures ARF6 activity or an ARF6-dependent recycling step under APOE4 vs APOE3. The "ARF6-related" part of H2 is therefore untested, not refuted. The one APOE4 vs APOE3 statistic on ARF6 found in round 10 is null and comes from a lipid-droplet fraction (42749735, not included). In isogenic human iPSC astrocytes, the ARF6 GAP ACAP3 is listed as lower in APOE4, with no value given, and ARF6 is not listed (37777962).
  2. **Human brain cells with their own APOE genotype.** No full paper measures receptor surface level, internalization or recycling in APOE3 vs APOE4 human iPSC astrocytes (or other human brain cells) carrying the genotype endogenously. The only such measure is a conference abstract (Kerman 2023, included): less ABCA1 at the cell membrane and in Rab11+ recycling endosomes in isogenic APOE4/4 human iPSC astrocytes, given as directions only, with total ABCA1 also lower. The other close papers are isogenic iPSC astrocytes compared only as APOE3 vs APOE2 (33837271, not included), total LRP1/VLDLR by western blot with no surface measure (34919811, not included), a whole-cell proteome that lists total ABCA1, VLDLR and recycling proteins as changed, by name only (37777962, included), and APOE-knockout human iPSC astrocytes given lipidated ApoE (Guo 2025, included). Direct surface and recycling measurements with endogenous APOE4 come from targeted-replacement mouse neurons (38217595) or from cells given exogenous ApoE4. The only human brain evidence from people without brain disease is McColl 2003: endosome-marker immunostaining in postmortem neurons from middle-aged controls, with no receptor measure.
     - *Human tissue instead of iPSC (round 7, 2026-11-20).* PubMed and Europe PMC full-text searches covered neurosurgical and epilepsy-surgery tissue, acute and organotypic human slices, fetal and primary human brain cells, acutely isolated human astrocytes and microglia, and postmortem brains from non-demented or young APOE4 carriers with endosome markers (420 new records). None measures receptor surface level, internalization or recycling by APOE genotype. The closest are Cataldo 2000 (10880397), where rab5 early endosomes are larger in APOE4 carriers than in APOE2/3 brains, but in aged brains with early AD pathology, with APOE2 pooled with APOE3 and endosome size as the only readout; and Roberts 2021 (34757788), a bulk-tissue protein screen in young APOE3/4 carriers with no receptor or ARF6 measure. Neither was included in round 7; Cataldo 2000 was added in the round 8 re-screen as class 3 endosome evidence, and Roberts 2021 stays out.
     - *Public datasets (round 6, 2026-11-19).* 28 public APOE3 vs APOE4 datasets (GEO, PRIDE, ArrayExpress) were screened for author-published statistics on ARF6, its regulators, the recycling machinery and the receptors. None gives such statistics outside papers already excluded, so no rows come from them. The orchestrator's own re-analysis of these tables is expression data only and is not part of the submission. In it, ARF6 shows no consistent change, and the two isogenic proteomes disagree on its direction.
  3. **Recycling rate.** Only three papers measure the rate of receptor recycling under APOE4 vs APOE3: slower insulin receptor recycling in mouse neurons given ApoE4 (Zhao 2017, Figure S8), unchanged transferrin recycling in isogenic human iPSC brain endothelial cells (Bell 2025, Fig 6D), and sortilin recycling halved with apoE4 in CHO cells, which are not brain cells (Asaro 2020, Fig 6E, with internalisation unchanged in Figure S5B). The rest of the recycling evidence is endosomal retention, Rab11 colocalization or steady-state surface level, which are consistent with a recycling block but do not measure one.
  - **No search found:** glutamate transporter (EAAT1/EAAT2) surface levels in APOE4 human astrocytes, an astrocyte cargo that would test H2 directly.
- **Exploratory kinetic model (2026-11-21; adds no rows and changes no values).** A compartment model of receptor trafficking (surface, early endosome, recycling endosome, degradation) was used to ask which mechanism the recycling evidence favours. Each APOE4 mechanism slows its own steps by one fitted factor: H2's ARF6 slow loop (recycling endosome to surface), an early-endosome trap (the NHE6 proposal), both together, or less endocytosis. APOE3 baseline rates and the APOE4 factor were fitted to means read off the only two published APOE4 vs APOE3 recycling time courses, and the mechanisms were compared by AICc.
  - Zhao 2017 (Figure S8A-B, insulin receptor, mouse neurons given recombinant apoE): the ARF6 slow-loop block fits best, with the slow loop about 5 times slower (factor 0.18), but only weakly (dAICc 2.6 over no APOE4 effect). The early-endosome trap fits worse than no effect. The fitted slow-loop model predicts surface/total insulin receptor at 0.75 of APOE3, close to the paper's own 0.74 (Fig 5G-H) and 0.88 (Figure S7B), which were not used in the fit. It also predicts total receptor about a third higher, whereas the paper reports total insulin receptor unchanged. In the model, slowing only the slow loop moves receptors inside the cell without lowering the absolute surface level.
  - Bell 2025 (Fig 6D, transferrin, isogenic human iPSC brain endothelial cells with endogenous APOE): in pair 1, every mechanism fits best with APOE4 recycling slightly faster (factor 1.4 to 2.0); in pair 2, no APOE4 effect fits best. Neither pair supports a recycling block.
  - Limits: 6 to 7 fitted parameters against 14 to 18 digitised points per dataset, so the rates are loosely determined, and fitting the Zhao recycling panel alone does not give a stable ranking. Neither dataset is from astrocytes. The model therefore supports gap 3 rather than closing it: the only kinetic data that show slower recycling come from mouse neurons with added ApoE4, where they are compatible with an ARF6-type slow-loop block, and the only human brain-cell kinetic data show none. The model, the digitised values and their figure sources are kept in the work folder (`models/sim/`), not in this repository.
- **Against or null findings:** 55 of 163. The strongest is a direct pulse-chase in isogenic human iPSC brain endothelial cells (Bell 2025, Fig 6D), which shows an unchanged transferrin recycling rate. Others:
  - unchanged surface Lrp1, Ldlr, insulin receptor and transferrin receptor in ApoE4-treated rat neurons (Xian 2018, Fig 2C);
  - unchanged Rab11 recycling endosomes in iPSC astrocytes (Narayan 2020);
  - unchanged ABCA1 in non-aged mice and in human cortex (Lee 2025);
  - equal surface LDLR with lipidated ApoE3 and ApoE4 in APOE-knockout human iPSC astrocytes (Guo 2025, Fig 2D; both are lower than with ApoE2, which is the paper's isoform effect);
  - unchanged Lrp1 and dentate-gyrus Apoer2 in ApoE4 targeted-replacement mice (Gilat-Frenkel 2014, Table 1), although CA1 and CA3 Apoer2 are lower;
  - in APOE4 targeted-replacement mouse neurons (38217595), a non-significant fall in surface insulin receptor (p = 0.07) and VEGF receptor (p = 0.15), and an unchanged Rab11 recycling-endosome area (Fig 7D, p = 0.56). The same paper reports significantly lower surface Apoer2 and LRP1 (Fig 4A, 4B), which count for H2;
  - equal uptake of ApoE3 and ApoE4 particles by rat astrocytes (Ralhan 2026, preprint);
  - no genotype difference in EEA1 early-endosome number or size in primary neurons from APOE knock-in mice, at two culture ages (Nyberg 2025, Fig 1C-F and 3D-G, one row per panel);
  - a non-significant rise in RAB5 in primary microglia from APOE targeted-replacement mice 24 h after a myelin challenge (Machlovi 2022, Fig 3d, p = 0.06); at baseline the rise is significant (Fig 1e);
  - unchanged sortilin internalisation with apoE4 in CHO cells (Asaro 2020, Figure S5B), where recycling of the same receptor is halved;
  - in APOE targeted-replacement mice (Nuriel 2017), no significant APOE4 vs APOE3 difference in Rab5a early endosomes at 6 and 12 months, the ages closest to the hypothesis's "non-aged"; the differences appear at 18 and 25 months, though average endosome size in entorhinal cortex is still not significant at 18 months (Fig 4C, p = 0.12);
  - a non-significant rise in EEA1 puncta in iPSC neurons from the second isogenic pair, a sporadic-AD APOE4/4 donor edited to APOE3 (Lin 2018, Figure S6E, P = 0.06), where the first pair shows a significant rise (Fig 2F) and total EEA1 rises in both;
  - in postmortem neurons from middle-aged people without brain disease (McColl 2003, n = 35 controls, APOE4 carriers vs non-carriers), rab4 recycling-endosome staining about 58% higher, not lower, in APOE4 carriers (Fig 6) and rabaptin-5 early-endosome staining about 13% lower (Fig 5), both read off the figure with no test within the controls (two-way ANOVA APOE4 main effect P = 0.62 for each). Higher rab4 could also mean slower exit from recycling endosomes, which the paper cannot separate. After global ischemia the rab4 rise is smaller in APOE4 carriers (interaction P = 0.017), which gives no row;
  - no difference in neuronal endosome volume by APOE genotype in advanced AD brains (Cataldo 2000, Results, data not shown, P = 0.112), where early-AD brains do differ;
  - higher, not lower, surface LRP1 with APOE4 in mouse neurons (Saroja 2022). That paper has no APOE3 arm (APOE4 is compared with no APOE and with APOE2), so its rows are class 5 with low relevance.
- **Effect sizes:**
  - Most figures print no percentage. Values read off bars carry `~` and `estimated_from_figure`.
  - Where the authors printed "~N%", it is recorded as printed, without the tilde, because the format reserves `~` for figure estimates.
  - A percentage-point difference in colocalization is recorded as the authors define it.
- **Model systems:** most evidence comes from mouse or rat neurons and astrocytes treated with exogenous ApoE, from ApoE-targeted-replacement mice, or from isogenic human iPSC cells. Each row's system cell records the system as facts (species, cell type, age, disease stage, how ApoE was supplied, background, comparator), following the challenge rule to record the system precisely and leave weighing its distance from the hypothesis to a later stage; an earlier draft added a "Deviation:" judgement to each cell, which was removed on 2026-11-21 with its facts kept. Lee 2025 rows from 18- and 22-month mice, and Nuriel 2017 rows from 18 and 25 months, are recorded as aged. Cataldo 2000 is postmortem tissue from aged brains with AD pathology. McColl 2003 is postmortem tissue from middle-aged controls (mean age about 50), 5 of 38 with amyloid deposits; like Cataldo 2000, it pools APOE2 carriers with APOE3, as each row states. Asaro 2020 and 2021 use CHO cells (Chinese hamster ovary), which are not brain cells, and their rows carry low relevance.
- **Statistic belongs to the contrast:** where a printed P value compares something other than APOE4 vs APOE3, it is not used. One example is Cheng 2026 Fig 4: p = 0.004 compares Rab5 with Rab7 within APOE4/4 cells. Such rows carry `N/A` with `not_found_in_source`, and the human_check note says why.
- **Shared donor:** both shared isogenic sets, Coriell AG09173 (APOE3 parental, edited to APOE4) and AG10788 (APOE4/4 parental, edited to APOE3), were made in Lin 2018, so Lin 2018 is not independent of the papers below. Coriell AG10788 (87 years, late-onset AD, APOE4/4) is the APOE4 parental line in Narayan 2020, the APOE4/4 line in Prasad 2018 (SI Appendix methods) and the APOE4/4 fibroblast line in Cheng 2026. Narayan 2020's APOE3 parental line, Coriell AG09173, is also one of the isogenic sets in 42239208. These papers are not independent of each other on the shared lines. Gilat-Frenkel 2014 and 38217595 come from the same laboratory and use the same ApoE targeted-replacement mouse lines, so they are not independent either. Machlovi 2022 uses the same targeted-replacement line family as Nuriel 2017 and 38217595 (different colonies, shared background), and de Leeuw 2025 comes from the same laboratory as Nuriel 2017. Nyberg 2025 uses separately built Jackson knock-in lines. 37777962 uses both Lin 2018 isogenic sets and a KOLF2.1J pair, without saying which pair its proteomics came from.
- **Not included:** papers on apoE particle recycling itself (not receptor recycling) were excluded, as were reviews.
